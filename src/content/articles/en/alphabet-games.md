**In short:** children learn letters faster through play than by staring at a chart. Pick one game a day, work on one or two letters at a time, and always say the letter’s **sound**, not just its name. If you’re not sure where to start, read the guide [how to teach the alphabet](/en/guides/how-to-teach-the-alphabet/) first.

## Games with no materials

**1. Letter hunt.** Pick a letter of the day and hunt for it on packaging, signs, in books and at the grocery store. Every letter found is a point. Count them up at bedtime.

**2. I spy with sounds.** The classic, with sounds instead of colors: “I spy with my little eye something beginning with mmm.” Your child looks around: milk, mom, mittens. Then it’s their turn.

**3. Clap for the sound.** Say a few words slowly and agree on a rule: when they hear the sound of the day at the start of a word, they clap. “Sun… dog… sock… sand.” Great for the car or a line at the store.

**4. Body letters.** Make letters with arms and legs, or with two people lying on the floor. I is easy, T too, X is arms and legs spread wide. Take photos and spell out your child’s name.

## Hands-on games

**5. Salt tray writing.** Pour salt, sand or cornmeal into a tray. Your child draws the letter with a finger, shakes the tray to erase and starts again. A great way to practice stroke direction without a pencil.

**6. Play dough letters.** Roll play dough “snakes” and lay them over a [letter tracing worksheet](/en/letter-tracing/). Then your child traces the letter with a finger.

**7. Mystery bag.** Put magnetic or foam letters in a cloth bag. Your child pulls one out without looking and guesses it by touch alone. Then they check and say its sound.

**8. Sound basket.** Choose a letter and ask your child to collect toys and objects that start with that sound: for B, ball, block, bear. If an odd one sneaks in, even better: talk about it.

## Paper games

**9. Big and small match.** Write capital letters on clothespins and lowercase letters around a piece of card. Your child clips each capital to its lowercase partner. It strengthens the pincer grip, too.

**10. Name puzzle.** Write your child’s name on a strip of paper, cut between the letters and mix them up. First they build it with the model, then without.

**11. Color and find.** Print a [coloring page](/en/coloring-pages/) with the letter’s character. Before coloring, your child finds the letter in the picture and circles it, then colors the letter in a single color.

## An outdoor game

**12. Letter hopscotch.** Draw letters on the ground in chalk. Say a sound, and your child jumps onto the right letter. For older kids, say a short word and they jump onto its first letter.

## Tips that make it work

- **One new letter at a time.** Games work best with 3 to 5 known letters and one new one.
- **Always the sound.** “Em” is the name; “mmm” is what your child needs to read.
- **Let them win sometimes.** And let them make up rules.
- **Stop before they’re tired.** Five happy minutes beat half an hour of whining.

Once your child recognizes several letters, move on to paper: the [letter tracing guide](/en/guides/how-to-teach-letter-tracing/) shows how. And the ABC Alphabet app has 12 games with the same letters and characters, spoken by native speakers.
